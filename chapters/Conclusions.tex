\chapter{Conclusions}

The aim of this research was that of enhancing the selection of $0\nu\beta\beta$ events while removing as many background-like events as possible. In order to do so it investigated and proved that deep learning models, exploiting the full time evolution of the current pulse, can match the state-of-the-art \ac{PSD} method of \ac{AE}, marking an important step forward in the use of machine learning techniques in rejecting \ac{MSE} in LEGEND-200. 

Two approaches were developed on ${}^{228}$Th calibration data of the \ac{IC} detectors V08682A and V08682B, using the \ac{DEP} of the 2614 keV line of ~${}^{208}$Tl and the \ac{FEP} of ~${}^{212}$Bi as \ac{SSE} and \ac{MSE} proxies: a supervised classifier built on the latent space of a \ac{VAE}, trained on peak-window labels contaminated by the Compton continuum, and an unsupervised \ac{DTC} model, which needs no labels. At the working point, where 90\% of the \ac{DEP} peak is kept, the \ac{VAE}-based classifier is statistically equivalent to \ac{AE} (Fig.~\ref{fig:summary-fep-sf}), and it becomes more efficient for looser cuts, especially for V08682B. The \ac{DTC} spontaneously separates \ac{DEP}-enriched and \ac{FEP}-enriched populations without any label, but rejects \ac{MSE} less efficiently than \ac{AE}, the best result being obtained with the \ac{CID} metric. The events on which the methods disagree suggest that the deep learning methods recognise the structure of the waveform, i.e.\ the number and the shape of its current peaks, but may not distinguish events that produce similar shapes for different physical reasons, such as unresolved \ac{MSE}, surface events and \ac{SSE} far from the p$^+$ contact. The performance of all the methods, \ac{AE} included, also depends on the detector geometry. Applied to $2\nu\beta\beta$ physics data, the deep learning cuts retain fewer events than \ac{AE}, with a rejection concentrated at low energy. Moreover the \ac{DTC} points out energy dependencies for two metrics out of three suggesting that the narrow energy window used for training may be the main limitation of the models.

Several developments are possible. The training should be extended to a wider energy range, to test whether the energy window is indeed the main limitation, and repeated to estimate its variability, and the study should include more detectors. Monte Carlo simulations of the calibration events, including the pulse shape, could also play a role. As discussed when introducing the two approaches in Chapter \ref{chapter:ML-PSD}, they are not reliable enough to train the models, which could learn features that are absent in real data. However, the knowledge of the true position and topology of each simulated interaction would make it possible to test the actual composition of events in the dataset. For the \ac{VAE}, the classifier could be trained on soft labels, given by the probability of each event to belong to the peak rather than to the continuum, could take also the standard deviations of the latent space as inputs, and could be merged with the \ac{VAE} into a single model that minimises the reconstruction and classification losses together~\cite{kingma2014semi}. For the \ac{DTC} the investigation of a higher number of clusters would be the first natural development, since in the energy window on which the training is performed, more than two types of events are expected; moreover the heatmap network of the original model~\cite{DTC} would show which part of the waveform drives the clustering and modifing the latent space into a variational one would avoid spending part of the algorithm capacity on the noise of the baseline. Finally, other approaches are worth exploring, from Gaussian mixture variational autoencoders~\cite{gmvae}, which unify representation learning and clustering, to simpler self-organising maps~\cite{kohonen1982}.

\begin{comment}
    Regarding the VAE-based classifier, the main limitation identified in this work is the quality of the labels. The classifier is trained to separate the DEP and FEP windows rather than SSE from MSE, and the purity of the two classes does not exceed 58\% and 76\% respectively (Tab.~3.3), since the Compton continuum under the peaks enters both samples. A natural development is to replace the hard window labels with per-event weights that express the probability of belonging to the peak rather than to the background. Such weights can be derived directly from the Gaussian-plus-background fit of the energy spectrum (Eq.~3.5), for instance with the \textit{sPlot} technique~\cite{sPlot}, so that the classifier learns to separate the DEP and FEP peaks themselves, i.e. genuine SSE from genuine MSE. In addition, the classifier currently operates on the eight latent means of a VAE trained for reconstruction, which are not optimised for discrimination. Training the encoder and the classifier jointly, or a supervised network acting directly on the current waveforms, would make it possible to establish whether the remaining gap with respect to the background-subtracted performance of A/E is due to the latent representation or to the physical limit set by the fraction of FEP events that are intrinsically single-site.    
\end{comment}

Moreover the \ac{TAE} has a deterministic latent space with no regularisation: any information that lowers the reconstruction loss can be stored in it, including the event-by-event fluctuations of the baseline. The reconstructions obtained after the pre-training suggest that part of this noise is indeed encoded, since small oscillations of the baseline are reproduced, whereas they are not reproduced by the \ac{VAE}, whose latent space is regularised by the \ac{KL} term. Encoded noise enters the distances between the latent sequences and the centroids, and can therefore degrade the clustering. A natural development is a variational version of the temporal autoencoder, in which the encoder outputs a mean and a variance for every time step of the latent sequence and a \ac{KL} term penalises the information stored in it, so that only the features that are relevant for the reconstruction of the pulse shape are kept. The clustering layer would then operate on the means of the latent sequences, combining the temporal structure of the \ac{DTC} latent space with the regularisation of the \ac{VAE}.
