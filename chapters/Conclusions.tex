\begin{comment}
    Regarding the VAE-based classifier, the main limitation identified in this work is the quality of the labels. The classifier is trained to separate the DEP and FEP windows rather than SSE from MSE, and the purity of the two classes does not exceed 58\% and 76\% respectively (Tab.~3.3), since the Compton continuum under the peaks enters both samples. A natural development is to replace the hard window labels with per-event weights that express the probability of belonging to the peak rather than to the background. Such weights can be derived directly from the Gaussian-plus-background fit of the energy spectrum (Eq.~3.5), for instance with the \textit{sPlot} technique~\cite{sPlot}, so that the classifier learns to separate the DEP and FEP peaks themselves, i.e. genuine SSE from genuine MSE. In addition, the classifier currently operates on the eight latent means of a VAE trained for reconstruction, which are not optimised for discrimination. Training the encoder and the classifier jointly, or a supervised network acting directly on the current waveforms, would make it possible to establish whether the remaining gap with respect to the background-subtracted performance of A/E is due to the latent representation or to the physical limit set by the fraction of FEP events that are intrinsically single-site.    
\end{comment}

Moreover the \ac{TAE} has a deterministic latent space with no regularisation: any information that lowers the reconstruction loss can be stored in it, including the event-by-event fluctuations of the baseline. The reconstructions obtained after the pre-training suggest that part of this noise is indeed encoded, since small oscillations of the baseline are reproduced, whereas they are not reproduced by the \ac{VAE}, whose latent space is regularised by the \ac{KL} term. Encoded noise enters the distances between the latent sequences and the centroids, and can therefore degrade the clustering. A natural development is a variational version of the temporal autoencoder, in which the encoder outputs a mean and a variance for every time step of the latent sequence and a \ac{KL} term penalises the information stored in it, so that only the features that are relevant for the reconstruction of the pulse shape are kept. The clustering layer would then operate on the means of the latent sequences, combining the temporal structure of the \ac{DTC} latent space with the regularisation of the \ac{VAE}.
